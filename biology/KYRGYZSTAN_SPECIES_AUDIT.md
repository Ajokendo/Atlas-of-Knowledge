# Kyrgyzstan regional species-evidence audit

**Status:** editorial audit of the seven-oblast flora and fauna chapters; not a national checklist, taxonomic monograph, or independent verification of every underlying record.

## Purpose and method

This audit checks whether the regional chapter structure distinguishes habitat context, dated observations, published locality records, historical reports and national-level checklist information. It uses the current committed text of `chapters/flora-kyrgyzstan.html`, `chapters/fauna-kyrgyzstan.html` and the shared `KYRGYZSTAN_BIODIVERSITY_DATA_STANDARD.md`.

A regional heading or a species mentioned in a regional narrative is not, by itself, proof of a verified occurrence at every named site in that oblast. Evidence labels should be applied to individual claims only when the cited source supports the same taxon, locality, date and method. Where the text does not provide that complete chain, the claim remains contextual or requires follow-up.

## Source-traceability limitation

The regional narratives and the chapter bibliography currently provide useful source material, but this audit does not treat the mere presence of a source in a chapter-wide bibliography as proof that it supports every adjacent species statement. Some regional claims still need a direct claim-to-source link or a compact citation immediately beside the relevant paragraph. This is a concrete editorial gap, not a presumed failure of the source itself. During the next species-account pass, each high-priority statement should be linked to the exact publication, official notice or dataset that supports its taxon, locality, date and method.

## Cross-cutting findings

1. **Seven-oblast structure is present in both chapters.** The regional framework is broad enough to guide future inventories, but it does not yet constitute seven complete, verified species checklists.
2. **The strongest claims are bounded observations.** Dated surveys, camera-trap events, official reserve notices and published plot studies should retain their date, locality, sampled area, effort and source.
3. **National and regional scales must remain distinct.** A national checklist or national population model cannot be presented as proof of presence or abundance in a particular oblast, reserve or valley.
4. **Historical and contemporary evidence must not be merged.** Historical Red Data Book references, Ramsar inventory figures and older fish assessments need an edition/year label and a current-source check before being described as current.
5. **Taxonomic uncertainty remains visible.** Synonyms, changing species concepts and provisional identifications should be recorded in the shared data standard; uncertain fish, amphibian, reptile and invertebrate totals should not be promoted to definitive counts.
6. **Sensitive localities require care.** Exact coordinates for threatened plants, nests, dens and vulnerable populations should be withheld or generalized in public-facing materials where disclosure could increase risk.

## Additional primary-source spot-check — regional vascular-plant records

**Seregin, Lyskov & German (2025), *Acta Biologica Sibirica* 11:551–579, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.15253859.** The paper reports results from a 2016 Moscow University expedition covering 90 sites over 28 days. The authors describe approximately 1,000 specimens in the introduction and 945 numbered gatherings / 963 physical specimens transferred to the Moscow University Herbarium in the methods; 397 specimens (37%) remained unidentified to species level by December 2016. The paper reports three taxa new for Kyrgyzstan, 31 new records for one of the seven floristic regions used in the national checklist, and 12 supplementary records. Its regional framework is **seven floristic/biogeographical provinces, not the seven administrative oblasts**.

**Talas/Besh-Tash example checked:** the article lists *Allium filifolium* from Besh-Tash Gorge, Talas Ala-Too, collected 23 July 2016 at 2,170 m (specimen MW0896287). The authors explain that the *A. kokanicum* complex has a difficult taxonomic history: an earlier broad synonymy was later revised to distinguish *A. filifolium*, while collections were also discussed under *A. kokanicum* sensu lato. The chapter therefore labels this as the determination used by the 2025 paper and explicitly retains the taxonomic caveat; it does not claim a newly verified modern population or a complete park list.

**Editorial consequence:** this is a valuable specimen-backed source for dated locality records and taxonomic context, but it must not be cited as a complete oblast-by-oblast inventory or treated as if each floristic province maps one-to-one onto an oblast. Where a chapter claim uses a named locality, retain the paper's actual locality, collection date, collector number / herbarium voucher and taxonomic caveats. The expedition is from 2016 even though the article was published in 2025. The article also notes that some taxonomically difficult collections were still unresolved; avoid implying that all collected material was identified.

Primary source: [Acta Biologica Sibirica article](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.15253859) · [full text](https://journal.asu.ru/biol/article/download/17262/14904/).

## National snow-leopard abundance estimates — comparison requiring caution

The 2026 *Snow Leopard Reports* article by Kubanychbekov et al. (5(1):26–33; DOI 10.56510/slr.v5.63883) summarises the PASK national assessment for 2020–2024. It reports 905 camera traps at 589 locations, 43 survey sessions, 372 detection events and 150 identified adult individuals. Spatial capture–recapture modelling estimated approximately **300 adult snow leopards**, with a 95% confidence interval of **246–366**. The article states that this is a model-based estimate, not a direct count. It also notes that the paper itself received editorial review but was not externally peer-reviewed.

A Ministry of Natural Resources notice dated **29 October 2026** gives a separate indicative national estimate of **450–550 snow leopards**. The notice does not provide the estimation method, confidence interval, survey period or age structure, so it is not possible from the published notice to determine whether it measures the same population component as the PASK adult-only estimate.

**Audit finding:** the two published figures are not directly comparable on the information currently available. The chapter now reports them separately, identifies their dates and source types, and explicitly marks the discrepancy as unresolved. Do not average them, silently replace the PASK model result with the Ministry range, or describe either figure as a direct census. Follow-up should seek the underlying Ministry estimate methodology / national monitoring report and confirm whether it covers adults only.

Sources: [Kubanychbekov et al. (2026), *Snow Leopard Reports*](https://doi.org/10.56510/slr.v5.63883) · [Ministry notice, 29 October 2026](https://mnr.gov.kg/ru/posts/news/v-dzeti-oguzskom-raione-na-video-zapecatleli-snezhnogo-barsa).

## Terskey Ala-Too expedition report — site-level evidence only

Zholdoshbekov & Hammer's Biosphere Expeditions report (published 24 June 2026; DOI 10.5281/zenodo.20821615) describes fieldwork from 7 July to 16 August 2025 in the Burkhan and Archaly valleys south of Issyk-Kul. The survey covered roughly 500 km² in 98 cells of 2 × 2 km over 40 days; 93% of cells were sampled only once.

- Snow leopard: recorded in eight cells. DNA analysis confirmed five of nine presumed scats, representing three individuals (two males and one female). Camera traps produced 112 photographs across eight cells and identified at least five individuals. These are different evidence streams; do not add the DNA and camera-trap identifications into a single total.
- Argali: recorded in only two cells, from a horn and one camera-trap photograph. This is a site-level survey result, not a national estimate or proof of a population decline across Kyrgyzstan.
- Pallas's cat: reported from two distinct cells. The Zenodo abstract contains the spelling *Otolobus manul*; the chapter uses the currently accepted genus spelling *Otocolobus manul* as listed by the Mammal Diversity Database, and notes the source typo rather than propagating it.

**Use limitation:** this is an expedition report involving citizen scientists, with most cells visited once. It is useful dated evidence of presence and local survey effort, but it is not a nationally representative inventory, a peer-reviewed abundance assessment, or a basis for extrapolating population trends. The chapter now preserves these boundaries in both languages.

Source: [Zholdoshbekov & Hammer (2026), Zenodo report 10.5281/zenodo.20821615](https://zenodo.org/records/20821615).

## Menzbier's marmot — survey estimate and two IUCN assessment frameworks

The Fauna & Flora International / Critical Ecosystem Partnership Fund final project report for “Building capacity to protect Besh Aral's meadows, marmots and megafauna, Kyrgyzstan” (project dates November 2020–February 2023; report dated 30 April 2023) describes comprehensive Menzbier's marmot surveys conducted in June 2021 and June 2022. The project reports an estimated **about 16,145 animals in the Besh-Aral area**, which it treats as the total Kyrgyzstan population. It compares this with an earlier estimate of about 12,000 in the same areas in 2002, but explicitly warns that the estimates are not fully comparable. The report also identifies one population of approximately 500–600 animals outside the reserve. These are project survey estimates, not a direct count of every animal; preserve the survey period and geographic scope.

The species' **IUCN Red List category is Vulnerable (VU)** in the 2023 assessment (Sultangaziev et al., DOI 10.2305/IUCN.UK.2023-1.RLTS.T12827A229617076.en). A separate IUCN Green Status assessment classifies recovery as **Critically Depleted**, with a recovery score of 13%, as summarised by the IUCN Small Mammals Specialist Group in March 2025. These frameworks answer different questions: Red List VU assesses extinction risk, while Green Status evaluates recovery. Do not substitute one label for the other or present “Critically Depleted” as a Red List category.

The fauna chapter now includes the project estimate, its comparability caveat, the Red List category and the separate Green Status recovery metric, with primary project and IUCN links.

Sources: [CEPF / Fauna & Flora International final report (30 April 2023)](https://www.cepf.net/resources/final-project-report/final-project-report-2747) · [IUCN Red List assessment (2023)](https://doi.org/10.2305/IUCN.UK.2023-1.RLTS.T12827A229617076.en) · [IUCN Green Status supplementary information](https://nc.iucnredlist.org/redlist/content/attachment_files/Green_Status_Supplementary_Information_12827_Marmota_menzbieri.pdf) · [IUCN Small Mammals Specialist Group, 28 March 2025](https://small-mammals.org/2025/03/28/green-status-update-2025/).

## Required evidence fields for future species accounts

Each account should record, where applicable:

- accepted scientific name, authority where useful, and synonyms;
- exact locality and spatial precision, with sensitive coordinates generalized publicly;
- date or date range and source publication / survey;
- evidence type (voucher, direct observation, photograph, camera trap, acoustic record, eDNA, published report or checklist);
- survey method and effort, including sampled area, route length, observation hours or camera-days;
- identification basis and confidence, including reviewer where available;
- whether the statement concerns presence, repeated detections, number of individuals, relative index or a population estimate;
- conservation category, assessment system and assessment year, kept distinct for national and IUCN classifications.

## Mammal checklist version 1.12 and bird-list scope comparison — 1 November 2026

### Central Asian mammals

GBIF records Adzhigildyaev P. (2026), *Checklist of the mammals of Central Asia*, version **1.12**, DOI [10.15468/hmrvjn](https://doi.org/10.15468/hmrvjn), published 26 October 2026 (GBIF metadata accessed 1 November 2026). It covers Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan and Uzbekistan and records country-level presence as resident, introduced, unconfirmed or absent. It includes species and subspecies and keeps global IUCN status separate from national Red Data Book entries.

The dataset metadata explicitly states that the checklist is a systematic synthesis of published literature, prioritising official national Red Data Book editions, then systematic monographs, specialist taxonomic papers and institutional checklists; IUCN, GBIF and Catalogue of Life are cross-checks. It is **not based on field sampling** and does not provide new site-level field evidence. The chapter bibliography was updated from version 1.11 to version 1.12 and now cites the current GBIF version and DOI.

Source: [GBIF dataset record and methodology](https://www.gbif.org/dataset/c71d02ff-b756-4cc0-b089-d29ba9155619) · [version 1.12 IPT resource](https://ipt.wingeds.world/resource?r=central_asia_mammals&v=1.12).

### Bird checklist totals are not directly comparable

Four public references currently produce visibly different Kyrgyzstan bird-list counts:

- The Birds of Kyrgyzstan country checklist lists **407 species** and presents a country-level checklist with dated rare-bird records.
- The OSME Kyrgyzstan country profile reports **327 species**.
- The Ornithology country database reports **380 species**.
- Limosa lists **133 species regularly represented in open observation data** and explicitly states that its list is incomplete and tends to omit scarce species.

These figures are not evidence of a real change in national species richness. The sources have different checklist purposes, inclusion criteria, taxonomic treatments and update histories. Limosa's 133 is specifically a regular-open-observation subset, not a national checklist. The chapter now explains this difference in both languages and deliberately avoids selecting, averaging or presenting one of these numbers as an independently verified definitive national total.

**Editorial rule:** use a checklist as a taxonomic or country-level reference index only after recording its version/date and scope. For a local occurrence claim, retain a dated primary observation, specimen, field report or other source that supports the named taxon and locality. A national checklist entry alone does not establish presence at every site or in every oblast.

Sources: [Birds of Kyrgyzstan checklist](https://kyrgyzstan.birding.day/v2checklist.php?l=en) · [OSME country profile](https://osme.org/country-profiles/kyrgyzstan/) · [The Ornithology country database](https://theornithology.com/country/kyrgyzstan/) · [Limosa open-data list and scope note](https://app.limosa.online/en/checklist/kgz).
