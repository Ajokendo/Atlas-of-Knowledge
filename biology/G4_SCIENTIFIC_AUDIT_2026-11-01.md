## Method

Reviewed regional occurrence and taxonomic claims against their cited primary/official sources and current specialist taxonomic references. Checked whether dates, survey footprints, methods, evidence types and uncertainty were preserved, and whether Russian and English passages express the same scope. Claims were not upgraded beyond what the cited evidence supports.

## Findings and decisions

### Fauna chapter

1. **Common Crane at Jety-Oguz (2026) — supported with appropriate qualification.** The Ministry notice dated 3 June 2026 reports a nest found on 19 April and a pair with two chicks observed on 24 May in the Jety-Oguz section of Issyk-Kul State Nature Reserve. It states that the previous national nesting record was in the Chui Valley in 1958 and that a reserve researcher's paper had been submitted to the Eurasian Crane Working Group bulletin. The chapter accurately attributes the record to the Ministry, distinguishes it from a population trend or range-expansion claim, and does not imply that the submitted paper was already published. No change required. Source: https://mnr.gov.kg/ru/posts/news/pervoe-gnezdovanie-serogo-zuravlya-na-vostocnom-pobereze-ozera-issyk-kul

2. **Bird taxonomy reference — verified.** AviList v2026 was published on 20 October 2026. Its own description identifies it as a coordinated taxonomic and nomenclatural checklist with revised range information; it is not evidence of a local Kyrgyz occurrence or a conservation assessment. The chapter states precisely these limitations. No change required. Source: https://www.avilist.org/checklist/v2026/

3. **Son-Kul fish survey (2023) — source discrepancies correctly retained.** The published article abstract describes field visits in 2021–2023, while its Materials and Methods section lists 2020, 2021 and 2023. The chapter flags this mismatch rather than silently selecting one date range. The article's fish material lists whitefish, peled, naked osman, scaled osman, Tibetan stone loach and lake minnow, while the chapter carefully distinguishes lake fish from the taxa reported in the Kok-Jerty outflow. Further verification of the exact locality assignment and vouchers remains desirable. Source: https://knau-bulletin.com/en/journals/tom-21-3-2023/nauchnyye-isslyedovaniya-v-rybolovstvye-na-primyerye-dvukh-promyslovykh-vidov-ryb-na-ozyerye-son-kul

4. **Suusamyr Valley fish survey (2024; published 2025) — numerical contradiction correctly flagged.** The article abstract reports four recreational-fishing species plus one weed fish; the body and conclusion state four fish species total. Its species table and text use the name `Triplophysa stoliczkai`. The chapter does not present the abstract's “4 + 1” as a settled total and retains the identification as the authors' determination pending diagnostic/voucher review. No unsupported reconciliation was made. Source: https://doi.org/10.36979/1694-500X-2025-25-12-159-166

5. **Fish taxonomy — current catalogue distinction verified.** Eschmeyer's Catalog of Fishes (species record updated 13 August 2026) treats `Triplophysa elegans` as valid and gives Kyrgyzstan in its distribution, while IUCN's assessment for `T. stolickai` uses a different taxonomic concept and its stated range does not include Kyrgyzstan. The chapter explicitly explains the conflict and does not silently merge the concepts. For `Gymnodiptychus dybowskii`, the current Catalog and ITIS treat that combination as valid and `Diptychus dybowskii` as a synonym/earlier combination. The chapter's cross-reference is appropriate. Sources: https://researcharchive.calacademy.org/research/ichthyology/catalog/fishcatget.asp?spid=53209 ; https://doi.org/10.2305/IUCN.UK.2022-1.RLTS.T168488A1186806.en ; https://researcharchive.calacademy.org/research/ichthyology/catalog/fishcatget.asp?spid=55960 ; https://itis.gov/servlet/SingleRpt/SingleRpt?print_version=PRT&search_topic=TSN&search_value=689192&source=to_print

6. **Maral and argali figures — scope safeguards retained.** The regional chapter distinguishes the Naryn Valley extrapolation from national statistics, keeps first-day argali observations separate from a full survey or national total, and preserves the article–Dryad provenance discrepancy for the genomic study. These are appropriate evidence boundaries; no numeric aggregation or inferred trend was introduced.

7. **Tibetan Snowcock and candidate Black-necked Crane records — appropriately cautious.** The chapter distinguishes the Himalayan Snowcock's documented Kyrgyz records from the unconfirmed range-edge claim for Tibetan Snowcock. The candidate Black-necked Crane report is not promoted to the confirmed national list. No change required.

### Flora chapter

8. **National vascular-plant total (4,043) — verified.** Sennikov et al. (2025) explicitly report a revised national checklist total of 4,043 vascular plant species after taxonomic corrections, ten new country records, one confirmed record and two exclusions. The chapter correctly dates the total and does not present it as a count of all organisms or a permanent final figure. Source: https://doi.org/10.1002/njb.04731

9. **Kulun-Ata flora totals — scope distinction retained.** The 2026 literature review reports approximately 394 higher-plant species based on older literature and explicitly warns that some taxonomic names are outdated. The chapter distinguishes this from a separate 2026 university expedition figure of 808 and does not treat either as a single settled, fully reconciled checklist. Source: https://ilim.uia.gov.kg/index.php/main/ru/article/view/917

10. **Aygul / Eduard's fritillary — correction made.** The chapter previously said that the plant is “now assigned to the genus `Petilium`” without stating the competing current taxonomic treatment. Kew's Plants of the World Online / World Checklist of Vascular Plants (2026) accepts `Fritillaria eduardii` and lists `Petilium eduardii` as its synonym. The local Kyrgyz source uses `Petilium eduardii`; to preserve the name used in that regional evidence and maintain continuity with the local source, the chapter retains it as the local-source name but now explicitly cross-references the accepted POWO name in both Russian and English. A direct link to POWO was added. This is a taxonomic clarification, not a change to the documented locality or conservation narrative. Source: https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/535120-1

11. **Besh-Aral, Besh-Tash and regional flora records — evidence scope appropriately qualified.** The chapter distinguishes 2022 plot monitoring published in 2025 from complete reserve checklists, KBA trigger taxa from whole-park inventories, and herbarium-supported records from unsettled taxonomic groups. No unsupported oblast-wide presence claim was identified in this bounded pass.
