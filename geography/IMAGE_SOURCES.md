## Status key

- **Pending search:** no specific candidate asset has been selected.
- **Candidate source:** a relevant institutional collection or educational resource has been identified, but no individual asset has passed rights and scientific review.
- **Rights checked:** the exact asset's licence/terms and attribution requirements have been recorded.
- **Approved:** science, rights, attribution, language and accessibility checks passed.
- **Integrated:** approved asset is committed to the site and its source metadata is recorded.

A site's general copyright statement is not a licence for every image on that site. Rights must be checked at the individual asset level. Public-domain status must also be confirmed for the exact media item, not inferred from the institution's name.

### Chapter 03 — ICS chart version and translation check

The official [ICS chart page](https://stratigraphy.org/chart/) identifies the current English chart as v2026/06, © International Commission on Stratigraphy 2026, and explicitly permits use under CC BY 4.0. The [ICS 2026/06 release note](https://stratigraphy.org/news/156) documents revised numerical ages for the base of the Anisian (247.0 Ma), Olenekian (250.8 Ma) and Wuchiapingian (259.857 ± 0.084 Ma); these are chart-version updates, not new measurements made by the atlas. The official [supplementary translations page](https://stratigraphy.org/supplementary) lists the available Russian translation as v2017/02, while the 2026/06 translation listing includes Azerbaijani. The atlas therefore retains the current English chart and supplies its own concise bilingual reading guide rather than presenting the older Russian chart as current. Numeric boundary ages remain subject to revision, as the chapter already states.

**Source checks accessed 5 November 2026.** USGS exact media pages identify the relevant Public Domain status; the USGS general copyright guidance also warns that some third-party material hosted on USGS pages may carry separate rights, which is why the Ore/Mineral composite remains under review. The ICS current-chart page identifies the exact version and CC BY 4.0 terms.

### Chapter 04 — data provenance for 4.13-A (Earth’s vertical extremes)

The diagram is a project-local SVG (provisional category C; individual creator/rightsholder, authoring method and project licence remain Unknown / PENDING). Its numerical claims are supported by the following sources:

- **Mount Everest:** Xie et al. (2022), *Determining the new height of Mount Qomolangma based on the International Height Reference System*, DOI [10.1080/10095020.2022.2128901](https://doi.org/10.1080/10095020.2022.2128901). Reports the 2020 snow-surface orthometric height of 8,848.86 m ± 0.06 m in the IHRS.
- **Challenger Deep, pressure-derived value:** Greenaway et al. (2021), *Revised depth of the Challenger Deep from submersible transects*, DOI [10.1016/j.dsr.2021.103644](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dsr.2021.103644). Reports 10,935 m ± 6 m at 95% confidence below mean sea level.
- **Challenger Deep, multibeam gridded estimate:** Fujii et al. (2026), *Full-ocean-depth EM124 mapping of the Challenger Deep by R/V Hakuho-maru and depth-estimate sensitivity tests*, DOI [10.1038/s41597-026-08267-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-026-08267-z). Reports a preferred eastern-basin gridded maximum of 10,927 m and a 10,914–10,932 m sensitivity range across five sound-speed models. The range is a model-sensitivity envelope, not a formal 95% confidence interval.

The current chapter text and SVG preserve the key distinctions: 2021 pressure-derived versus 2026 multibeam-gridded methods; the 2026 preferred value is not presented as a definitive replacement; the sensitivity range is identified separately; and the graphic states that the vertical comparison is not drawn to one linear scale. Scientific/data-claim review for 4.13-A: **PASS** for the values and qualifications checked against these sources. Rights for the SVG itself and technical rendering remain PENDING.
