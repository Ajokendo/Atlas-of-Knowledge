# AUTHOR-CREATED VISUALS STANDARD
## Authorship, provenance, licensing, and verification of original project visuals

**Document ID:** AOK-IMG-C-001  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Mandatory companion standard  
**Applies to:** Every current and future Atlas of Knowledge discipline and every project-created visual asset  
**Governing policy:** `IMAGE_FIRST_POLICY.md`  
**Implementation guide:** `VISUAL_IMPLEMENTATION_GUIDE.md`  
**Canonical location:** Repository root — `AUTHOR_CREATED_VISUALS_STANDARD.md`  
**Owner:** Atlas of Knowledge central project governance

## 1. Purpose and scope

This standard governs visuals made specifically for Atlas of Knowledge rather than simply found and reused. It establishes how the project records authorship and provenance, chooses and documents a licence, reviews the asset, integrates it, and earns the project status `VERIFIED`.

It applies to, among other things:

- original SVG, PNG, JPEG, WebP, GIF, and other image files;
- diagrams, scientific plates, maps, charts, graphs, infographics, and timelines;
- original photographs, drawings, illustrations, and 3D renders;
- animations, simulations, interactive models, and visual components generated by code;
- AI-assisted or AI-generated visual assets;
- language variants, mobile layouts, responsive variants, and other derivatives of an original project visual.

A visual may be original even when it is made with familiar tools or uses established scientific conventions. Conversely, a file is not automatically original merely because it is stored in the project repository, has a new filename, is exported as SVG, or has been translated.

This standard supplements the universal `IMAGE_FIRST_POLICY.md`. It does not replace the image-first search requirement, the general rights rules, or the subject-level `IMAGE_SOURCES.md` and `IMAGE_AUDIT.md`. If there is a conflict, the universal policy takes precedence. This standard adds stricter provenance and verification requirements for Category C assets.

## 3. What qualifies as a project-created visual

A visual may be assigned **Category C — Created** only when its visual content was produced specifically for the project and is not merely an unchanged or modified third-party asset.

Examples may include:
- a diagram composed from scratch in SVG;
- an original educational illustration drawn for a chapter;
- a chart generated from project-authored code and a documented dataset;
- a map newly composed from identified, properly licensed source data;
- an original 3D model or animation;
- an AI-assisted image whose source inputs, human direction, review, and rights position have been recorded.

The following do not, by themselves, establish Category C:
- a file is present in a subject's assets folder;
- the file has a project-specific filename;
- an image has been converted to SVG, PNG, or another format;
- surrounding text, caption, alt text, or interface has been translated;
- a screenshot has been taken from a third-party work;
- a third-party image has been cropped, recoloured, annotated, or redrawn;
- code renders or animates a pre-existing third-party visual;
- an AI tool generated an output without a documented provenance and rights review.

If a visual contains both original and third-party elements, classify and document the components separately. If the relationship is uncertain, keep the category **PENDING** until the lineage is established.

## 4. Required authorship and provenance record

Before a Category C visual can pass verification, its subject register must identify the asset and explain how it came into existence.

Record at least:

1. **Stable visual ID** — unique within the subject atlas (for example, `CH-V12`).
2. **Asset family** — the conceptual visual and all its files/variants. For example, an English SVG, Russian SVG, and mobile SVG layouts may be one visual family with separately listed files.
3. **Learning purpose** — the exact concept or skill taught.
4. **Files and locations** — every source file, exported file, language version, responsive version, and interactive dependency.
5. **Category and method** — Category C plus a precise method, such as hand-authored SVG, code-authored SVG, original drawing, original photography, GIS composition, 3D modelling, animation, or AI-assisted illustration.
6. **Human contribution** — who specified the learning objective, directed or edited the work, checked its content, and approved it for project use. Do not invent a human creator where none is established.
7. **Tools and services** — relevant software, libraries, AI systems/model identifiers where available, and creation date. A tool provider is not automatically the legal author or rightsholder.
8. **Inputs and source materials** — prompts or design brief where relevant, reference images, datasets, fonts, icon sets, templates, code libraries, textures, and other incorporated materials, with their own source and rights records.
9. **Originality statement** — what was independently created and what, if anything, was reused, traced, adapted, or generated from third-party input.
10. **Evidence** — editable source, design files, source code, commit, creation log, prompt/design brief where relevant, and a stable repository path.

Keep original editable/source files whenever practical. A final exported image alone may be insufficient to establish the construction history of a complex diagram or interactive asset.

For AI-assisted work, disclose the AI contribution accurately. Record the tool/service and date, the task or prompt summary, material reference inputs, the human direction and edits, and the human review performed. Retain the full prompt or generation record when available and useful for reproducing or investigating the asset. Do not claim that AI output is legally copyrightable, exclusively owned, or automatically licensed merely because the project requested or edited it; the legal position can depend on the applicable law, tool terms, and human contribution.

## 5. Licensing and permission for project-created visuals

### 5.1 No automatic licence

Category C does **not** automatically mean that the project may assign any licence it chooses. Before publication, the project must establish who has authority to license the asset and whether all included components can be distributed on the proposed terms.

Do not infer ownership or licensing authority from:
- the fact that an assistant or AI system generated the file;
- the fact that a team member saved it in GitHub;
- the fact that the project paid for or has access to a tool;
- a repository-level licence that may not cover every embedded asset;
- the absence of a visible copyright notice.

If authority or rights are unclear, record **Unknown / PENDING** and do not mark the rights check PASS.

### 5.2 Choose a licence deliberately

For each original asset or clearly defined asset family, record the exact licence and version, a link to its legal text, the licensor/rightsholder authorised to apply it, and the date of approval.

The project may choose an appropriate open licence after confirming authority and compatibility. Examples include:
- **CC BY 4.0** — attribution required; commercial reuse is permitted under the licence terms.
- **CC BY-NC 4.0** — attribution required; use is restricted to noncommercial purposes under the licence terms.

These are examples, not automatic project defaults. The Physics team's use of CC BY-NC 4.0 for four new diagrams is a precedent to document and review, not a blanket licence for every Physics asset or for any other discipline. Do not apply CC BY-NC 4.0 to Chemistry or another subject without a separate, explicit licensing decision.

The chosen licence must match the project's intended sharing and reuse model. A noncommercial restriction is not equivalent to a general free/open licence and may limit downstream educational, institutional, or commercial uses. Do not promise that an asset is unrestricted or “free for any use” when its licence contains conditions.

### 5.3 Mixed and third-party components

An original visual may include third-party fonts, icons, textures, photographs, maps, datasets, scientific models, code, or other components. Record each material component and its licence separately. Confirm that its terms permit the intended combination, modification, and distribution.

Do not place a third-party component under a project licence unless the project has the necessary rights. If a component cannot be cleared, replace it, remove it, obtain permission, or keep the whole visual out of publication.

### 5.4 Required licence evidence and attribution

The register must contain:
- exact licence name and version, or the written permission reference;
- the licence text URL or permission evidence;
- the authorised licensor/rightsholder;
- date checked and person responsible;
- exact attribution/credit line to display;
- any required notices and a list of material changes;
- the scope of files covered by the licence.

If one conceptual visual has multiple language or layout variants, explicitly list every covered file. Do not assume a licence statement for one SVG automatically covers an unlisted derivative, a separate source file, or third-party content embedded in it.

A repository-wide `LICENSE` file does not silently override a more specific asset-level licence. Where the two appear inconsistent, stop and resolve the conflict before publication.

## 6. Required verification procedure for Category C

Every project-created visual must pass the checks below. A successful upload, an automated test, a creator's own approval, or a licence label is not enough.

### Gate 1 — Identity and provenance

Confirm:
- the visual ID and asset family are stable;
- all files and variants are listed;
- category C and the actual creation method are justified;
- source/editable files or other reasonable creation evidence are retained;
- AI use and all material third-party inputs are disclosed;
- no inherited or adapted component is being misrepresented as original.

**Evidence:** source files, commit/path, creation record, inputs and provenance notes.

### Gate 2 — Rights and licence

Confirm:
- the person or entity applying the licence has authority to do so;
- tool/service terms and relevant rights have been considered;
- every third-party component is cleared for the intended use;
- the selected licence is explicit, accurate, and approved;
- every covered file/variant is identified;
- the required attribution and notices are prepared.

**Evidence:** licence text or permission, approval record, component-level source list, exact credit line.

If the rightsholder, authority, licence, or component rights remain unknown, this gate is **PENDING**, not PASS.

### Gate 3 — Scientific, factual, and pedagogical review

A competent subject-matter reviewer must check:
- factual or scientific correctness;
- labels, formulae, symbols, terminology, units, axes, scales, proportions, directions, relationships, and data;
- the match to the stated learning objective and learner level;
- the distinction between measured fact, conceptual model, simulation, reconstruction, and artistic illustration;
- simplifications, uncertainty, exceptions, and limitations;
- absence of misleading visual emphasis or scientifically false implications.

The reviewer must record concrete findings, not merely “looks correct”.

**Internal review:** the creator must not be the sole approver of their own visual. A second reviewer, distinct from the creator and competent in the relevant subject matter, must perform and document the scientific/factual review. This reviewer may be part of the Atlas of Knowledge team; an external appointment is not required for internal verification. If no suitably competent second reviewer is available, keep the scientific review PENDING and do not claim `INTERNALLY VERIFIED` or `VERIFIED`.

**External review:** independent review by a qualified specialist who is not part of the asset's production/approval chain is an additional quality-assurance level. It is recommended for high-impact, technically complex, safety-relevant, or especially sensitive visuals, but it is not a universal prerequisite for internal verification or publication. Record the expert's identity/credentials, scope, date, findings, and outcome separately. External review must never be claimed unless it actually occurred.

**Evidence:** review checklist or comments, reviewer identity/role, date, corrections, and outcome.

### Gate 4 — Language and terminology

For every supported language, check:
- captions, labels, legends, annotations, embedded text, and controls;
- correct subject terminology, spelling, grammar, units, and symbols;
- equivalent educational meaning and level of detail;
- correct reading order and no clipped or untranslated text;
- consistency between the visual and the chapter explanation.

A translated file is a variant of the asset family, not proof that the underlying visual is scientifically verified.

**Evidence:** language checklist, reviewer/date, corrections, and final rendered versions.

### Gate 5 — Accessibility and learner usability

Check:
- meaningful alternative text in each supported language, or a documented reason why the visual is decorative;
- text explanation, long description, or data/table equivalent for complex visuals;
- sufficient contrast, readable type, and legibility at common screen sizes and zoom levels;
- no reliance on colour alone;
- accessible labels and keyboard operation for interactive content;
- visible focus, logical operation, and screen-reader names/descriptions where applicable.

**Evidence:** accessibility checklist and manual test results. Automated metadata checks alone are not sufficient.

### Gate 6 — Technical integrity

Check:
- all files parse and load;
- paths, references, dependencies, and variants resolve;
- dimensions, viewBox, aspect ratio, and responsive layout are correct;
- no broken images, clipped labels, overlap, overflow, or unreadable details;
- mobile and desktop layouts are tested;
- interactive components work with expected inputs and do not produce relevant errors;
- source/editable files and generated outputs are reproducible where applicable.

**Evidence:** CI/test result, browser/device checks, commit SHA, and known limitations.

### Gate 7 — Integration and final approval

Confirm:
- the correct subject page uses the approved final file;
- the exact integrated file matches the reviewed version;
- captions, alt text, source attribution, and licence notices are visible or accessible as required;
- `IMAGE_SOURCES.md` and `IMAGE_AUDIT.md` agree;
- all six prior gates are PASS, or justified NOT APPLICABLE where genuinely relevant;
- the final reviewer and approval date are recorded.

Only after this gate may the visual be marked `VERIFIED` (the machine-readable lifecycle status for **internal verification**). External expert review is recorded separately and may support the additional designation `EXTERNALLY VERIFIED`; it is not a prerequisite for `VERIFIED`.

## 7. Statuses and review results

Use the lifecycle statuses defined by `IMAGE_FIRST_POLICY.md`: `MISSING`, `FOUND`, `LICENSE_CHECK`, `SCIENTIFIC_CHECK`, `ADAPTED`, `INTEGRATED`, `VERIFIED`, and `REJECTED`. In reporting, describe `VERIFIED` as **INTERNALLY VERIFIED**. Use **EXTERNALLY VERIFIED** only when a documented independent external specialist review has also passed; this is an additional assurance designation, not a replacement for the underlying lifecycle status.

For Category C, also maintain separate check results in the audit:

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Authorship and provenance | PASS / FAIL / PENDING / NOT APPLICABLE |
| Rights and licence | PASS / FAIL / PENDING / NOT APPLICABLE |
| Scientific/factual/pedagogical review | PASS / FAIL / PENDING / NOT APPLICABLE |
| Language and terminology | PASS / FAIL / PENDING / NOT APPLICABLE |
| Accessibility and usability | PASS / FAIL / PENDING / NOT APPLICABLE |
| Technical integrity | PASS / FAIL / PENDING / NOT APPLICABLE |
| Integration and final approval | PASS / FAIL / PENDING / NOT APPLICABLE |

Use `NOT APPLICABLE` only with a short reason. Do not use it to bypass a difficult check.

A Category C visual may be `INTEGRATED` while checks remain open. It may not be `VERIFIED` / **INTERNALLY VERIFIED** unless every applicable internal gate is PASS and evidence is recorded, including a competent second reviewer distinct from the creator. External specialist review is an optional additional level: its absence does not block internal verification or publication. A visual may be designated **EXTERNALLY VERIFIED** only after that external review is documented as PASS. A rights PASS does not prove scientific accuracy; scientific PASS does not establish licensing authority.

If a review finds a defect, correct the asset and repeat the affected checks. If provenance or rights cannot be established, do not publish it; replace it, seek permission, or keep it rejected/pending. External review is not a substitute for rights clearance or internal checks. An asset that has passed all internal gates may be published without waiting for external review. If internal scientific review is still PENDING, it must not be presented as verified; any exceptional preliminary/draft publication requires explicit central approval, clear provisional labelling, cleared rights, and documented risk assessment.

## 8. Asset families, language variants, and responsive versions

A single educational visual may have several physical files:
- English and Russian variants;
- desktop and mobile layouts;
- light/dark versions;
- static and animated exports;
- source/editable and production files.

Give the conceptual visual one stable visual ID and list all associated files and their roles. Record which files are public outputs and which are working/source files. Do not inflate the number of distinct educational visuals by counting every responsive or language variant as a separate learning concept.

At the same time, do not hide files from the rights record: each published variant must be explicitly covered by the relevant licence and review. If a variant changes meaning, data, labels, or scientific content, review that change and record its own check results.

## 9. Required subject-level records

Every discipline keeps its own `IMAGE_SOURCES.md` and `IMAGE_AUDIT.md` inside its assigned subject directory. These files must link to this canonical standard rather than copy it.

For each Category C visual, the source register must include the fields in section 4 and the licence/attribution details in section 5. The audit must include all seven gates from section 6, their results, evidence, reviewers, dates, and unresolved actions.

Suggested concise record:

| Field | Example / instruction |
|---|---|
| Visual ID | `CH-V12` |
| Category | `C — Created` |
| Asset family | English + Russian SVG; desktop/mobile variants |
| Method | Code-authored SVG; AI-assisted authoring |
| Creator/contribution | Record actual human and AI contributions accurately |
| Tool/date | Tool/service and creation date |
| Source inputs | Original brief, reference materials, data, fonts, libraries |
| Source evidence | Editable file, commit, creation log |
| Licence | Exact approved licence/version, or Unknown/PENDING |
| Licensor authority | Evidence of authority to apply the licence |
| Files covered | Every public variant and its path |
| Attribution | Exact credit line |
| Review gates | Seven check results with evidence |
| Final status | INTEGRATED or VERIFIED, as justified |

## 10. Versioning and change control

A material change to an approved visual creates a new review obligation. Examples include changes to scientific content, data, labels, units, scale, source inputs, embedded text, or a third-party component. Re-run the affected gates and update the audit. Do not retain `VERIFIED` automatically after a material change.

Purely technical changes that do not affect content (for example, a lossless file optimisation) still require a technical check and traceable commit; document why other review gates remain valid.

The central project owner maintains this standard in the repository root. Subject teams may add stricter discipline-specific checks in their own audit procedures, but may not weaken this standard or create competing universal copies. Changes to this standard must be reviewed through the central integration process.

## 11. Applying this standard to existing assets

This standard applies retrospectively to existing Category C candidates and to local assets whose origin is uncertain.

For an existing asset:
1. inventory every file and group genuine variants into an asset family;
2. establish whether it is inherited, adapted, or project-created;
3. retrieve creation evidence, source files, commits, prompts/design briefs, and relevant team records where available;
4. record creator/contributor, tools, inputs, and rights authority without guessing;
5. choose and document an explicit licence only after authority and included components are checked;
6. complete the seven verification gates;
7. correct the source register and audit together;
8. leave unresolved items PENDING and do not claim full verification.

A retrospective search can help identify alternatives and sources, but it cannot prove that an earlier visual was originally created from scratch or that a search-first process was followed at the time. Record retrospective findings as retrospective.

## 12. Final rule

**A project-created visual is not verified because we made it. It is verified only when its origin is traceable, its rights are established, its educational content is independently reviewed, its language and accessibility are checked, its implementation works, and the evidence is recorded.**

The `VERIFIED` status is a project quality-control decision. It is not a legal opinion, a guarantee of copyright ownership, or a substitute for expert review.
